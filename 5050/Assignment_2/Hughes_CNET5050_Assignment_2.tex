If Q\_k is the expected probability of our edge attaching to any vertex
with a degree k, then the average probability of our neighbor being is
the degree distribution times this probability.

\[\sum_{k}{k}*Q(k) = \sum_{k}{k} * \frac{kP(k)}{\langle k \rangle} \rightarrow\]
\[= \sum_{k}{} * \frac{k^2P(k)}{\langle k \rangle}\]
\[= \frac{\langle k^2 \rangle}{\langle k \rangle}\]

    If \(\sum_k{k}P(k)=\langle k \rangle\),
\(\sum_k{k^2}P(k)=\langle k^2 \rangle\), and \(P(k)=Ck^{-\gamma}\)

Then:

\[\langle k \rangle = \sum_{k=k_{min}}^{k_{max}}{k}Ck^{-\gamma}\] and,
\[\langle k^2 \rangle = \sum_{k=k_{min}}^{k_{max}}{k^2}Ck^{-\gamma}\]

which is equal to:

\[\langle k \rangle = C \sum_{k=k_{min}}^{k_{max}}{k^{-\gamma + 1}}\]
and,
\[\langle k^2 \rangle = C \sum_{k=k_{min}}^{k_{max}}{k^{-\gamma + 2}}\]

Since, \(P(k)\) must equal 1 to reflect our probability, we define C as
\[C= \frac{1}{\sum_{k=k_{min}}^{k_{max}}k^{-\gamma}}\]

That gives us:

\[\langle k \rangle = \frac{\sum_{k=k_{min}}^{k_{max}}{k^{-\gamma + 1}}}{\sum_{k=k_{min}}^{k_{max}}k^{-\gamma}} \]
and,
\[\langle k^2 \rangle =  \frac{\sum_{k=k_{min}}^{k_{max}}{k^{-\gamma + 2}}}{\sum_{k=k_{min}}^{k_{max}}k^{-\gamma}} \]

for, \[C= \frac{1}{\sum_{k=k_{min}}^{k_{max}}k^{-\gamma}}\]

    The friends of a randomly selected node have a higher mean degree than
the nodes themselves because, hubs which have the highest degrees
definitionally connect to more nodes than a normatitive node. This
means, that as a normatitive node we are more likely to connect to a
hub, since k\_hub is simply greater than k\_normatitive. In other words,
we tend to connect to hubs simply because they have more opportunities
for connections writ-large, and therfore our neighbors tend to be higher
degree than ourselves. The alternative is some kind of flat-degree
distribution where either everyone is a hub or no one.
